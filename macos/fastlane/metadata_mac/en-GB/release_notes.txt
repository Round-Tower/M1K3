NEW IN 1.2.1

• I rest when you do. Leave me alone for about 90 seconds, with nothing in the box and nothing on the go, and the companion behind your chat fades out and stops drawing. Hover, click, type or scroll, or let me start listening, thinking or speaking, and I'm back. Measured on an M1 Max with the chat window in front: idle CPU fell from about 46% to near zero. Your battery and fans should notice. Same in the menu bar and the brain picker; Reduce Motion skips the fade.

IN CASE YOU MISSED 1.2

• Smoother thinking. Lil keeps its thinking to a set budget and answers straight after, so a hard question never runs away. The live thought streams smoothly as it grows.
• A lighter Constellation. The memory field behind your chat draws as a few shapes instead of a thousand, at about a third of the CPU it took before.
• Lil wakes fast. Switching brains no longer leaves the old one warming up in the background, and the progress never freezes at 0%.
• Quicker voice with Big. Big keeps your last few exchanges close, about four seconds faster per spoken turn, and still remembers what you said earlier.
• I hear names better. In voice mode I fix a misheard name towards the people and places already in my memory.
• Better memory answers. Ask what you decided or told me and I look in your knowledge first, not the web.
• Memory capture off now means never. Turns you send with it off are never kept, even after a relaunch.
• A straight answer about privacy. Ask what leaves your Mac and I lead with the truth: some of it can, when web search or the private cloud is on.

NEW TO M1K3? HERE'S WHAT I DO

• Three brains: Mini (instant, on Apple Intelligence), Lil (4B, sees images) and Big (12B). Pick the one that suits your Mac.
• Talk to me: on-device speech recognition and a natural neural voice.
• Bring your own knowledge: drop in notes and PDFs and I answer from them, on your Mac.
• I remember what matters to you, in a memory you can see, search and delete.
• Call memory: with your consent, I transcribe and summarise calls, encrypted on your Mac.
• Brain at Home: pair your iPhone or iPad over your own Wi-Fi and use your Mac's brain from the sofa.
• Your coding agents can use my knowledge and voice through a local MCP server and the m1k3 command-line tool.
• Siri and Shortcuts can ask me things and add to what I know.
• Private Cloud Compute, if you want it: off by default, one message at a time, with your consent.

Everything else runs on your Mac. No account, no telemetry. The network is used for model downloads, web search and the optional private cloud. Settings > Brains & downloads names every download; web search and the private cloud are switches in Settings > Privacy.
